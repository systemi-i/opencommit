# ICSL Lineage and Rationale

Status: candidate draft.  
Date: 2026-07-07.

## 1. Why ICSL Exists

Public-service delivery, regulation, institutional administration, and collective governance
increasingly depend on software systems. Yet much of the governance logic remains trapped in
policy documents, workflow diagrams, forms, local interpretations, vendor systems, and tacit
organizational practice.

ICSL exists to make institutional commitments explicit, portable, auditable, comparable,
and executable without collapsing governance into mere compliance checklists.

## 2. Institutional Grammar Lineage

ICSL stands downstream of institutional grammar work, especially Elinor Ostrom's work on
institutions as rule-structured systems and the Crawford/Ostrom grammar of institutional
statements.

Institutional grammar asks how rules are composed: actors, attributes, deontics, aims,
conditions, and sanctions. ICSL inherits the insight that institutions have grammar and that
governance can be represented more precisely than prose alone allows.

ICSL differs in its operational ambition. It asks how institutional grammar becomes a
runtime commitment system: how rules create accepted institutional effects, what evidence
opens a gate, who has authority to bind, what version applies, what recourse exists, and what
receipt remains.

## 3. From Rules to Commitments

The central ICSL move is to treat the binding institutional act as the atomic unit.

Workflow systems usually center tasks. Legal systems often center texts. Case-management
systems often center records. AI systems often center reasoning.

ICSL centers the CommitmentPoint: the moment an institution accepts, denies, certifies,
modifies, delegates, remedies, supersedes, or closes a reliance-worthy institutional effect.

This shift matters because it lets different systems interoperate around institutional
meaning rather than local workflow labels.

## 4. Governance as Operational Heart

Traditional digital delivery often treats governance as compliance after the fact.

ICSL flips that orientation. Governance becomes the operational heart: the thing that shapes
what execution is allowed to do, when it is allowed to commit, what evidence it needs, who
can bind, what version applies, what remedies exist, and what memory must be retained.

This is why ICSL is potentially a foundation for an internet of interoperable public
services and institutional actions. It creates a shared protocol layer for institutional
effects.

## 5. Interoperability and Comparability

ICSL supports interoperability by making the following portable:

- commitment points
- authority semantics
- evidence requirements
- dependency logic
- recourse states
- version pins
- receipts
- conformance claims

It supports comparability by making institutional processes inspectable across domains
without forcing every jurisdiction or agency into the same policy.

## 6. Necessity

As AI-assisted administration, public-service automation, cross-agency delivery, digital
identity, benefits systems, climate adaptation, infrastructure coordination, and regulated
service ecosystems expand, institutions need a way to encode not just workflows but binding
governance semantics.

Without a layer like ICSL, systems will continue to interoperate at the level of APIs, data
fields, and documents while disagreeing about authority, recourse, institutional effect,
version, and accountability.

That gap is likely to become more costly as automation grows.
