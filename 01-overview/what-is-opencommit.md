# What Is OpenCommit?

OpenCommit is an open initiative to make public-service rules, protocols, commitments, and decisions easier to coordinate, audit, reuse, and trust.

It is developing ICSL, the Institutional Coordination and Service Language: a candidate open standard for representing governed public action in a structured, portable, and verifiable form.

## The Problem

Public systems can exchange data, verify identity, trigger workflows, and move payments. But they still struggle to exchange the meaning of public action.

Most systems cannot reliably answer:

- Who had authority to act?
- What rule applied?
- What evidence was used?
- What commitment or obligation was created?
- What recourse was available?
- Can another system understand, verify, or rely on what happened?

Without this, digital public infrastructure can move faster but become harder to govern. AI can increase capacity but blur authority. Systems can exchange data but fail to preserve agency, rights, and institutional meaning.

## The OpenCommit Approach

OpenCommit turns public services into open, versioned protocols.

Protocols define the governed process: the roles, rules, evidence, authority, dependencies, versions, and recourse pathways.

Commitments are the moments when a protocol creates real institutional meaning: an approval, denial, obligation, referral, inspection result, payment duty, deadline, sanction, appeal window, or safeguard requirement.

Receipts make those actions traceable: what happened, under which protocol version, with what authority, using what evidence, and with what recourse.

## The Endgame

OpenCommit aims to support an open governance network: an internet of interoperable institutional actions and public services.

This would allow societies to coordinate and execute at network speed while preserving legitimacy, autonomy, agency, accountability, and trust.
