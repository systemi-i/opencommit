# What v0.1 Claims and Does Not Claim

This scope statement is authoritative for the ICSL v0.1 candidate and is adopted by [ADR-007](../07-decisions/ADR-007-v0.1-scope.md). Public explanations that conflict with it should be treated as documentation defects.

## What v0.1 Specifies

ICSL v0.1 specifies a candidate model for publishing institutional protocols, defining CommitmentPoints and their gates, accepting Commitments in governed contexts, recording Receipts, deriving later status, packaging artifacts, and making qualified conformance claims.

Where the specification defines canonicalization, identity, hashing, and structural validation, independent implementations are expected to reproduce the same canonical bytes, identifiers, hashes, and mechanically defined validation results for the same conforming JSON value and declared validation context. Reproducibility begins after semantic authoring; it does not imply that independent encoders will choose equivalent representations of the same institutional source.

## What v0.1 Does Not Establish

**No certification of legal correctness.** ICSL does not determine whether an encoding faithfully represents applicable law, policy, or practice. Encodings may be incomplete or wrong.

**No legal authority by syntax.** A protocol, CommitmentPoint, or Receipt does not create authority merely because it conforms to the schema. Authority comes from the relevant institutional and legal source.

**No override of authoritative sources.** Where an encoding conflicts with the source it represents, the source prevails and the encoding is defective.

**No complete workflow specification.** ICSL specifies governable institutional action and evidence. It does not necessarily specify task order, interfaces, staffing, transport, retries, or other end-to-end orchestration details.

**No proven production interoperability.** Independent implementations and cross-language vectors have not yet demonstrated production interoperability.

**No universal determinism.** Deterministic evaluation is possible only where the underlying protocol is determinate. Discretion and professional judgment must be represented rather than converted into artificial rules.

**Receipts are rebuttable evidence.** A Receipt records what an institution claims to have accepted. It is not itself a legal instrument or proof of substantive legality.

**Unsigned receipts in v0.1.** Hash chaining provides tamper evidence relative to an externally retained chain head. Authentication, non-repudiation, signatures, and key governance are outside v0.1.

**Qualified conformance only.** Every claim must identify its subject identity, subject type, class, ICSL version, test-suite version, and result. A `pass` requires a suite profile for the named subject type. Unqualified “ICSL compliant” claims are not supported.

## Purpose of the Boundary

ICSL aims to make institutional acts machine-verifiable without pretending that institutional legitimacy can be reduced to syntax. These limits preserve that distinction and define what evidence future versions and implementations must add.
