# Civic Agents and AI Boundaries

Civic agents are AI-enabled or software-mediated tools that help people and institutions navigate public processes.

They might help a resident apply for a service, help a caseworker understand required evidence, route a person to the right program, summarize a protocol, prepare an appeal, or check whether a decision satisfies required gates.

## The Boundary

OpenCommit does not treat civic agents as institutional authorities by default.

A civic agent may assist, explain, draft, route, summarize, or validate. It should not silently decide, bind, deny, approve, or remove recourse unless a governed protocol explicitly authorizes that behavior and the relevant gates are satisfied.

## Why This Matters

AI is moving into public administration quickly.

Without explicit boundaries, AI can blur authority, evidence, and responsibility.

OpenCommit helps make those boundaries operational:

- what the agent may help with;
- what the agent may not decide;
- what authority is required;
- what evidence must be durable;
- what record must be produced;
- what recourse remains available.

This is how civic agents can become useful without becoming unaccountable.
