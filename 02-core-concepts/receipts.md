# Receipts

Receipts are the auditable records of institutional action.

A receipt records what happened, under which protocol version, with what authority, using what evidence, producing what outcome, and with what recourse.

## Why Receipts Matter

Receipts allow public-service actions to become portable and verifiable.

They help answer:

- What action occurred?
- Which protocol version governed it?
- Which commitment point was involved?
- What evidence was used?
- What authority acted?
- What outcome was created?
- What recourse was available?
- Can another system rely on this action?

## Network Effect

Receipts are what allow one system, agency, auditor, civic agent, or downstream service to understand and rely on an action created elsewhere.

They are a trust mechanism for an open governance network.
